What the current page asks of someone

With one quest charted, the Planner is about 2,600 px tall and shows, top to bottom: an eyebrow, a title, a two-line blurb, a count line, search, a Refresh button, a Popular goals fold, three filter chips, the board (two cards with their own buttons), a switcher, Tree/Route, the quest card with its own four buttons and three tabs, a requirements grid, a quests grid, an Opens up column, four stat tiles, an eight-row accordion of skills, a quest-point gates line, then the full tree with a legend and three controls, then the route.

That is every answer at once. You read it fine because you know which bits to skip. A newer player reads all of it and feels tested.

The rules both directions follow

  • One question per screen. A: "what should I do for this goal?" B: "what can I do right now?" Everything else is a tap away, not a scroll away.
  • Say the reason, not the data. "Underground Pass first" instead of a grid of eight requirements with you-values.
  • Only the gaps. Levels you already have aren't listed. Levels the chain's rewards hand you aren't listed either, one footer line says so.
  • Counts are counts, not buttons. "56 you could start today" is a sentence under the title. The filter chips go.
  • One header. Title plus one line. No eyebrow, no italic blurb, no "data from" line (that moves to Settings).
  • The tree is a place you go, full screen, not a wall at the bottom of every page. It's the part people like once they're in it, so keep it, but behind a row.
  • Colour from the game. Skill icons carry the colour. The app's own palette stays to three jobs: accent, done, blocked.
  • Grouped lists, hairline rules, chevrons. The iOS Settings shape. It reads as "a few things" even when it's twenty.

A, one goal at a time

Goals as a strip of cards with rings. Pick one and the page becomes that goal: three things to do next, the levels you still need, and a short More list. The serif title keeps it Mortimeter. Best for someone with one or two goals, which is most people.

B, your list

Route first, goal second. Everything pinned melts into one ready-now list, each row saying which goal it serves. Waiting rows say the one thing standing in the way. Bold sans title, closer to a plain iOS app. Best for someone with a big board who just wants to be told what to click.

They aren't exclusive: A is the goal screen, B is the home of Map, and tapping a goal pill in B opens A.
